Q4 budget pacing: how to not run out of money on Black Friday

Out-of-budget at 2pm on the highest-converting day of the year is the most expensive mistake in Amazon advertising, and it is entirely preventable. Here is how we pace November.

Every November we audit accounts that did everything right except one thing: they ran out of budget. Campaigns went dark at two in the afternoon on Black Friday, the traffic kept coming, and the sales went to a competitor whose budget was still live.

This is the most expensive mistake in Amazon advertising, and it is also the most preventable. It is not a bidding problem. It is a pacing problem, and the fix starts in early November, not on the day.

Why pacing beats bidding in November

For most of the year, the lever that matters is the bid. You are managing cost per click against conversion rate, and budget caps are a background concern because traffic is predictable.

November breaks that assumption. Traffic volume moves far more than conversion rate does. A campaign that comfortably spent its daily budget by midnight in October will hit the same cap by late morning on Black Friday, because the number of shoppers searching has multiplied while your cap has not.

The consequence is subtle and worth being precise about: when a campaign exhausts its budget, it stops serving entirely. You do not get reduced impressions, you get none. Your best-converting campaign, the one with the lowest ACoS in the account, goes dark at exactly the moment its traffic is most valuable.

The core idea

In Q4 your constraint stops being cost per click and becomes hours of coverage. A campaign at 40% ACoS that runs all day will usually out-earn a campaign at 25% ACoS that runs until lunchtime.

The three-tier budget structure

Before November starts, sort every campaign into one of three tiers. This takes an afternoon and it is the single highest-value piece of Q4 preparation.

Tier 1: protected

Branded campaigns, your best-performing exact match campaigns, and anything defending a hero ASIN. These should be set to budgets they cannot realistically exhaust, typically three to four times their October daily spend. You are not trying to spend that money. You are buying insurance against going dark.

Tier 2: scaled

Proven non-branded campaigns inside target ACoS. Raise these to roughly double October levels, and plan to review them every two or three days through the month. This is where most of your incremental Q4 revenue comes from.

Tier 3: capped

Research campaigns, broad match discovery, new product launches. Keep these flat or reduce them. November is an expensive month to run experiments, and the clicks you buy here are the most expensive clicks of the year. Discovery work belongs in September or January.

What to do in the week before

The week before Black Friday is for removing obstacles, not for optimising. Specifically:

  • Check every campaign end date. Campaigns set to end on 30 November are more common than you would think, and they fail silently.
  • Confirm your payment method has headroom. A declined card during peak is a catastrophic, entirely administrative failure.
  • Verify inventory against forecast. Advertising a product that goes out of stock on the 29th wastes the spend and damages rank.
  • Check the Budget Rules you set last year. Rules scheduled for last year’s dates can fire at the wrong time or not at all.
  • Stop making structural changes. Campaigns need data to stabilise. Restructuring on 20 November means going into peak with campaigns that have no performance history.

Use the breakeven ACoS calculator before you raise anything, because the budget you can justify depends entirely on where your margin actually sits. Holiday pricing and promotional discounts change that number, and plenty of sellers raise bids in November against margin assumptions from July.

Black Friday and Cyber Monday hour by hour

The operational pattern that works is simple and mostly consists of not panicking.

  • Early morning: check nothing has gone dark overnight. Confirm spend is tracking against a normal share of daily budget.
  • Mid-morning: this is the first danger window. Any campaign that has spent more than half its daily budget before lunch will exhaust before evening. Raise its budget now, not later.
  • Afternoon: repeat the check. Resist the urge to adjust bids on the day. Same-day ACoS figures are unreliable because attribution lags, and you will be reacting to noise.
  • Evening: evenings convert well. Make sure Tier 1 and Tier 2 still have room to run.
Attribution warning

The ACoS you see on Black Friday afternoon is not the ACoS you will see on Tuesday. Sales attribute back to clicks over following days, so in-day figures always look worse than reality. Judging performance mid-event and slashing bids in response is one of the most common and most costly Q4 errors.

The mistakes that cost the most

Ranked by what we see cost accounts the most money:

  1. Going dark on peak days. Everything above exists to prevent this.
  2. Cutting bids mid-event on incomplete attribution data. You suppress your own traffic at the worst possible time.
  3. Raising bids without re-checking margin. Promotional pricing lowers your breakeven ACoS, so the bid that was profitable in October may not be in November.
  4. Launching new campaigns in late November. They have no data, no history, and compete with your proven campaigns for budget.
  5. Forgetting that December exists. Accounts that spend everything in the Black Friday window frequently underperform through a December that is longer and, in aggregate, often larger.

None of this is complicated. It is mostly preparation, done early, followed by a disciplined refusal to make clever decisions during the event itself.
