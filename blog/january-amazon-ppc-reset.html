The January reset: what to fix before Q1 gets away from you

Q4 generated the best data your account will see all year. January is when it is complete enough to act on, and when you have the quiet to do it properly.

Q1 Planning· 27 October 2026· 8 min read
In this article

  2. Run the search term audit now
  3. Rebuild what Q4 distorted
  4. Reset budgets honestly
  5. Handle the returns wave

Q4 produces more search term data in eight weeks than the rest of the year produces in six months. It is also, while it is happening, the worst possible time to act on it.

January is when that changes. Attribution has settled, the numbers have stopped moving, and there is enough quiet to do structural work without risking peak revenue. This is the most valuable month of the year for PPC work, and it is routinely wasted on nothing in particular.

Why January is the right moment

Three things line up. Q4 attribution is finally complete, so what you are looking at is real rather than provisional. Traffic has returned to predictable levels, so changes produce readable results. And the cost of being wrong is at its lowest point of the year.

Make a structural mistake in November and it costs you peak revenue. Make the same mistake in January and you have eleven months to recover from it.

Run the search term audit now

This is the core of the reset. Pull the search term report for the whole of Q4 — the entire period, not the last 30 days — and work through it systematically.

You are looking for four groups:

  • Converters you are not targeting directly. Terms that produced orders from auto or broad campaigns and deserve their own exact match. This is where most of the upside sits.
  • Spenders that never converted. Terms with meaningful clicks and no orders across the entire quarter. With a full quarter of data, a term with no orders is a genuine finding rather than a small sample.
  • Terms that only worked at Q4 volume. Some terms convert during peak and never otherwise. Keep them, but do not budget for them in February.
  • Irrelevant matches. Broad and auto campaigns drift. Q4 volume makes the drift visible.

The keyword decision tool handles the judgement for individual terms, including the part people get wrong — whether a zero-order term has actually had enough clicks to be called a loser, or is simply under-tested.

One caution

Do not negative-match aggressively based on Q4 alone. Shopping behaviour in November is not typical behaviour. A term that failed during peak, when buyers were purchasing gifts, may perform perfectly well in March. Negative the clear and persistent losers; leave the ambiguous ones for a decision in April.

Rebuild what Q4 distorted

Peak trading leaves structural debt behind. Common items:

  1. Emergency budget increases never reversed. Campaigns quietly running at four times their justified spend because of a Black Friday decision nobody revisited.
  2. Placement adjustments set for peak competition. A Top of Search adjustment that made sense in November is usually wrong in January. Recalculate it with the placement bid calculator.
  3. Campaigns created in haste. Duplicates, overlapping targeting, campaigns competing with each other for the same keyword.
  4. Dayparting rules from the event. Budget rules written for Cyber Monday that are still firing.

Reset budgets honestly

Daily budgets set in November are nearly always wrong for January, and the direction of the error is consistently the same: too high, inherited rather than chosen.

Rather than scaling everything down by a flat percentage, rebuild from margin. Pricing often changed over Q4 — promotions, coupons, fee changes in the new year — so start by recalculating breakeven and target ACoS with the breakeven calculator, then set budgets to match what that target can actually support.

Note also that Amazon fee changes typically take effect early in the year. A target ACoS calculated in December may be wrong by February for reasons that have nothing to do with your advertising.

Handle the returns wave

January brings returns, and returns distort the picture in ways that are easy to misread.

  • Your real ACoS is worse than reported. Advertising reports attribute the sale, not the refund. A high-return product is less profitable than its ACoS implies.
  • Check return rate by ASIN before scaling. Scaling spend on a product with a 20% return rate is scaling a loss.
  • Returns reveal listing problems. A spike in “not as described” returns usually means the listing is overselling. That is a conversion problem that advertising cannot fix and will only amplify.

Work through those five areas properly and the account enters February with current data, honest budgets and a structure that reflects how it actually performs — which is a materially better position than most accounts manage.
